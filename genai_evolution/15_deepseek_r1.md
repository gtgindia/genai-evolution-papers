# DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning (DeepSeek-AI, 2025 — arXiv 2501.12948)

## The story in one paragraph
Big chat models could write fluently and follow instructions, but they still stumbled on hard math, code, and logic that needs many careful steps. Chain-of-thought prompting had shown that asking a giant model to think aloud helps, yet those chains were hand-written by humans and only worked for very large models. DeepSeek asked a bolder question: what if we never teach reasoning by example at all, and instead let it emerge from pure reinforcement learning with only a final-answer checker. Starting from their strong DeepSeek-V3-Base, they rewarded only correct boxed math answers, passing code tests, and correct format, and watched the model invent long self-checking thought, backtracking, and reflection on its own. After polishing readability with a four-stage pipeline and then distilling the skill into small models, the resulting DeepSeek-R1 matched OpenAI o1 on math and code while being open-weight and dramatically cheaper, proving that reasoning can be incentivized, not just imitated.

## What problem was hurting before this paper
Instruction tuning and human preference tuning had made models polite but not deeply logical. Supervised fine-tuning on human-written chains scales poorly because experts must write thousands of step-by-step solutions, and the model merely copies their style without learning to verify itself. PPO with neural reward models added cost and hacking: a learned judge can be fooled, needs its own training data, and needs a value network as large as the policy to estimate long-term reward, which is especially hard when an early sentence looks wrong but later gets corrected. Prompted chain-of-thought also cost many tokens at test time and only emerged past about 100 billion parameters, leaving small open models behind. Closed reasoning models like o1 showed strong results but hid their methods and charged high prices. The field needed a transparent recipe that teaches true multi-step verification with minimal human labeling and that can be compressed into affordable models.

## The core idea, explained simply (with an analogy)
The core idea is to reward only the destination and let the model discover its own road. Imagine a maze where you never give a child a map, you only ring a bell when they exit correctly and require them to narrate their walk inside think tags. At first the narration is short and messy. Over many tries the child learns that longer detours with checking, undoing bad turns, and trying alternatives ring the bell more often, so the narration grows into careful self-talk with wait, let me recheck moments. DeepSeek-R1 does exactly this. R1-Zero starts from base with only a tiny template that says put thinking inside think tags and final answer after, then learns purely from rule rewards for right math, passing code, and clean format. Once reasoning emerges, R1 adds a little human polish for readability and helpfulness, and finally teaches small students by letting them read 800 thousand polished reasoning stories from the big teacher, which works far better than making small models explore the maze alone.

## How it actually works (step by step: architecture, training objective, inference — explain each piece in words; explain key equations in plain language)
The base is DeepSeek-V3-Base, a 671-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts with only 37 billion active per token, pretrained on 14.8 trillion tokens mostly in English and Chinese, with multi-head latent attention for memory-efficient long context and multi-token prediction for faster training. No new Transformer is proposed here; the novelty is training.

Step one is R1-Zero with pure reinforcement learning and no supervised warm-up. The prompt is minimal: user question, then assistant must write thinking inside think tags followed by the answer. The reward has only two equal parts: accuracy reward which is one or zero from a rule checker, boxed math matched with sympy, code compiled against hidden tests, multiple-choice matched exactly, and format reward which is one or zero for having correct think tags. Deliberately there is no neural reward model to avoid hacking and retraining cost. Optimization uses GRPO, group relative policy optimization, instead of PPO. For each question the old policy samples a group of 16 answers, each gets a rule score, and advantage is the score minus group mean divided by group standard deviation, so good-in-group answers are pushed up and bad-in-group pushed down with no value network. A small KL term with coefficient 0.001 keeps the policy near a reference that is refreshed every 400 steps to allow exploration. Training details are large: learning rate 3e-6, temperature 1.0, batch 512 with 32 unique questions, 10,400 steps, max length grown from 32 thousand to 65 thousand tokens, 8192 rollouts per step. In plain language, GRPO compares siblings rather than asking a separate critic to guess future reward, which is vital when a long chain revises itself.

Step two is the aha-moment. Mid-training on a square-root equation, the model spontaneously writes wait, wait, that is an aha moment I can flag here, let us reevaluate step by step, in first person, then backtracks and fixes itself. Word counts of wait spike, average length grows toward 20 thousand tokens, and AIME accuracy climbs from 15.6 to 77.9 percent. No one taught reflection; it was incentivized because rechecking earns more bells.

Step three turns hard-to-read Zero into friendly R1 with four stages. First cold-start supervised tuning on thousands of filtered Zero traces, rewritten by humans into conversational first-person and translated to match question language, yielding R1-Dev1 that is readable but temporarily dips on reasoning. Second reasoning-oriented RL on 26 thousand math plus 17 thousand algorithm plus bug-fix, STEM, and logic problems with an added language-consistency reward that counts words in target language, yielding R1-Dev2 that recovers math and code. Third rejection sampling plus second supervised tuning: sample Dev2 widely, keep 600 thousand good reasoning traces judged by V3 plus 200 thousand non-reasoning helpful examples, 800 thousand total, fine-tune to R1-Dev3 that jumps on AlpacaEval and code repair. Fourth all-scenario RL with combined rule reward for reasoning plus learned helpful and safety reward models that score only the final summary, plus language reward, at temperature 0.7 for coherence for 1700 steps, yielding final R1 that gains 25 points on AlpacaEval and 17 points on ArenaHard with math preserved.

Step four is distillation for the community. The 800 thousand R1 traces are used for pure supervised tuning of six small students, no RL, for two to three epochs at 32 thousand context: Qwen2.5-Math 1.5B and 7B, Qwen2.5 14B and 32B, Llama-3.1 8B, and Llama-3.3 70B Instruct. Key lesson: a distilled 32B beats the same 32B trained with 10 thousand RL steps directly, 72.6 versus 47.0 on AIME, because small models cannot explore efficiently but can imitate a strong teacher cheaply.

At inference time you use zero-shot prompting, not few-shot, because few-shot degrades reasoning, and you let the model emit a long think block followed by a concise summary answer.

## Key results and numbers from the paper (with the actual scores/tables described in words)
Reasoning emergence is dramatic. R1-Zero alone lifts AIME 2024 pass-at-one from 15.6 percent for base V3 to 77.9 percent, with majority vote over 16 samples reaching 86.7 percent above human average, and Codeforces rating from 1134 to 1444.

Final R1 matches the closed frontier. On AIME 2024 it scores 79.8 percent, essentially tied with o1 at 79.2 and far above o1-mini at 63.6, V3 at 39.2, and GPT-4o at 9.3. On MATH-500 it reaches 97.3 percent above o1 at 96.4. On Chinese CNMO it reaches 78.8 versus 67.6 for o1-mini. On GPQA Diamond science it reaches 71.5 below o1 at 75.7 but above all others. On Codeforces it reaches 96.3 percentile with rating 2029, tied with o1 at 96.6 and 2061, far above V3 at 58.7 percentile. On LiveCodeBench it reaches 65.9 above o1 at 63.4. On SWE-Verified engineering it reaches 49.2 tied with o1 at 48.9. Helpfulness also jumps through the pipeline: AlpacaEval from 55.8 to 87.6, ArenaHard from 53.6 to 92.3, IF-Eval instruction following from 46.6 to 83.3, while MMLU stays at 90.8 near o1 at 91.8.

Distillation democratizes reasoning. Distill-Qwen-32B reaches AIME 72.6, MATH 94.3, LiveCode 57.2, Codeforces rating 1691. Distill-14B reaches 69.7 on AIME, 7B reaches 55.5, even 1.5B reaches 28.9 on AIME and 83.9 on MATH, beating GPT-4o and Claude on math despite tiny size. Distill-Llama-70B reaches AIME 70.0 and MATH 94.5 with Codeforces 1633.

## Why this paper matters (what later work it unlocked — name the specific later papers among the 15 where relevant)
This paper matters because it proved reasoning need not be hand-taught but can be grown with verifiable rewards, and then cheaply shared. Among the 15 papers, it builds directly on paper 09 on chain-of-thought, turning eight handwritten exemplars into twenty-thousand-token learned self-verification, on paper 08 on InstructGPT, replacing human-preference PPO with rule-based GRPO without value networks, on paper 03 on GPT-3 few-shot learning, showing zero-shot reasoning beats few-shot, on paper 10 on Chinchilla, using massive token training to make base strong enough for RL, on paper 12 on FlashAttention-style efficiency and mixture-of-experts sparsity to afford 65 thousand-token thinking, and on paper 14 on DPO as the contrasting simplification that R1 moves beyond for logic. It crowns paper 13 on LLaMA 2 open weights by going fully MIT-licensed with distilled Qwen and Llama students anyone can run, and it closes the loop to paper 04 on RAG, paper 05 on DDPM, paper 06 on CLIP, and paper 07 on latent diffusion, because future agents will combine retrieval, vision, generation, and now reliable reasoning.

## Honest limitations
R1 still cannot use tools, browse, or run a calculator during thinking, so it must do arithmetic internally and sometimes overthinks simple questions with thousands of tokens. Output structure is weak on JSON or strict formats, prompt sensitivity remains with few-shot hurting scores, and language mixing between English and Chinese appears outside the trained distribution despite a language reward. Safety and writing still need human data and short RL, because rule rewards only cover math and code with checkers. Software-engineering RL was limited by slow evaluation, and pure RL needs reliable verifiers, so domains without clear checkers cannot use Zero directly. Finally, long chains cost latency and money, motivating distillation but also showing that thinking time must be budgeted wisely.

## Key terms explained (glossary of 5-8 terms)
Reinforcement learning means learning by trial and error from a reward bell rather than by copying examples. GRPO means group relative policy optimization, which scores a group of answers to the same question and pushes up better-than-average siblings without needing a separate value critic. PPO means proximal policy optimization, an older RL method that needs a value model and careful tuning of future-reward estimates. Rule reward means a checker that gives one for correct boxed math, passing code tests, or clean format and zero otherwise, with no learned judge to hack. Aha-moment means the spontaneous emergence of self-reflection phrases like wait, let me recheck, learned because backtracking earns more reward. Cold-start means a small readable supervised warm-up before RL so later thinking is both smart and pleasant to read. Rejection sampling means generating many answers and keeping only correct and readable ones for training. Distillation means teaching small student models by supervised tuning on traces from a big teacher, which beats making small models explore alone.

## Connections (which earlier paper it builds on, which later paper builds on it)
This paper builds on paper 09 on chain-of-thought, which invented think-aloud prompting that R1 learns to generate at length, on paper 08 on InstructGPT, whose PPO with KL penalty it replaces with GRPO and rule rewards, on paper 03 on GPT-3, whose decoder pretraining and zero-shot evaluation it extends, on paper 10 on Chinchilla, whose token-rich training makes base capable of long RL, on paper 12 on FlashAttention and mixture-of-experts efficiency that afford 65 thousand-token contexts, on paper 14 on DPO as the simpler preference alternative that cannot teach math verification, and on paper 13 on LLaMA 2 open chat that it surpasses with fully open reasoning.
