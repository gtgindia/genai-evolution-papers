# Training Language Models to Follow Instructions with Human Feedback / InstructGPT (Ouyang et al., OpenAI, 2022 — arXiv 2203.02155)

## The story in one paragraph
GPT-3 could write fluently, but it often missed the point of what the user actually wanted. It would make up facts, continue a question with more questions, or produce toxic text, because it was only trained to predict the next word on the internet, not to be helpful. The OpenAI alignment team hired about 40 contractors and taught GPT-3 manners in three steps: first show it good answers written by humans, then have humans rank several answers to teach a separate reward model what good looks like, and finally use reinforcement learning to nudge the language model toward high-reward answers. The resulting InstructGPT models were so much more helpful that a tiny 1.3-billion-parameter InstructGPT was preferred over the giant 175-billion-parameter GPT-3, and this exact recipe became the secret sauce behind ChatGPT.

## What problem was hurting before this paper
Large models were misaligned with user intent. Predicting the next token on web pages rewards confident continuation, not honesty or obedience, so models hallucinated facts, followed the wrong instruction, ignored explicit constraints like write in two paragraphs, and produced biased or sexual or violent content. Prompt tricks helped a little, but even few-shot GPT-3 still frustrated customers on the OpenAI API who wanted a helpful assistant. Public instruction datasets like FLAN and T0 covered mostly classification and question answering, while real API use was dominated by open-ended generation and brainstorming, so fine-tuning on those public sets did not fix real usage. The field needed a general way to inject fuzzy human values like helpful, honest, and harmless into a pretrained model without rewriting the whole internet training set.

## The core idea, explained simply (with an analogy)
The core idea is to treat human preference as a teachable score. Imagine training a puppy: first you demonstrate the trick, then you give treats for better attempts and withhold treats for worse ones, until the puppy internalizes what pleases you. InstructGPT does the same with three roles. Human demonstrators show ideal responses for supervised warm-up. Human rankers compare several model responses and say which is best, which trains a reward-model judge that can score any new response automatically. Finally, the language model practices generating answers and gets treats from the judge, adjusting its policy with reinforcement learning while staying close to its original behavior so it does not go crazy chasing points. Mixing in a little of the original pretraining keeps general knowledge intact, like reminding the puppy of old tricks while learning new ones.

## How it actually works (step by step: architecture, training objective, inference — explain each piece in words; explain key equations in plain language)
All models start from GPT-3 transformer decoders at 1.3, 6, and 175 billion parameters, with a 2000-token context window.

Step one is supervised fine-tuning, called SFT. The team collected about 13,000 prompts, mostly English, from the API Playground and from labelers who wrote plain, few-shot, and user-based instructions, filtered for personal information and deduplicated by user. Labelers wrote ideal demonstrations, and GPT-3 was fine-tuned on them for 16 epochs with cosine decay and dropout. Although validation loss overfit after one epoch, human preference kept improving with longer training, so the checkpoint was picked by reward-model score.

Step two is reward-model training, called RM. For about 33,000 prompts, the SFT model and variants generated 4 to 9 answers each, and labelers ranked them from best to worst. A 6-billion-parameter model, initialized from SFT with its final word-prediction layer replaced by a single-number score head, was trained to give higher scores to preferred answers. In plain language, the loss says: for every pair where answer A was preferred over answer B, push the score gap wider so the model assigns high probability to that human choice. To avoid overfitting from correlated pairs, all pairs from one prompt were treated as one batch element, requiring only one forward pass per completion. Scores were then shifted so demonstrations averaged to zero.

Step three is reinforcement learning with PPO. The SFT policy interacts with a bandit environment: it receives a random customer prompt without labels, writes a response, and gets a scalar reward from the frozen reward model minus a small per-token KL penalty that punishes drifting too far from the SFT model. This penalty prevents reward hacking where the model spouts nonsense that fools the judge. The PPO algorithm updates the policy for 256,000 episodes with batch size 512, using a 6-billion-parameter value function initialized from the reward model. The default PPO-ptx version adds a pretraining term that also asks the model to keep predicting normal internet text well, weighted by a coefficient gamma around 27.8 and a KL coefficient beta of 0.02, which the authors found removed the alignment tax on academic benchmarks without hurting preference. At inference time there is no extra machinery: the user just prompts the final InstructGPT model and samples normally, because the helpful behavior is now baked into its weights.

## Key results and numbers from the paper (with the actual scores/tables described in words)
Human preference gains were large and consistent. Outputs from 175-billion-parameter InstructGPT were preferred to 175-billion-parameter GPT-3 about 85 percent of the time, and preferred to few-shot prompted GPT-3 about 71 percent of the time. Remarkably, the small 1.3-billion-parameter InstructGPT was already preferred over the 175-billion-parameter GPT-3, despite having 100 times fewer parameters. Labelers also judged InstructGPT more appropriate as a customer assistant, better at following explicit constraints, less likely to miss the instruction entirely, and less likely to hallucinate on closed-domain tasks, where made-up information fell from about 41 percent for GPT-3 to about 21 percent for InstructGPT.

On TruthfulQA, human raters judged InstructGPT truthful and informative about twice as often as GPT-3. On toxicity with RealToxicityPrompts, InstructGPT produced about 25 percent fewer toxic outputs when asked to be respectful, though with no prompt the gap vanished, and when explicitly asked to be toxic it became more toxic than GPT-3, showing it follows instructions faithfully. Bias on Winogender and CrowS-Pairs did not improve by entropy measures. On public NLP sets like SQuAD, DROP, HellaSwag, and French-to-English translation, vanilla PPO regressed, but PPO-ptx recovered most of the loss and even beat GPT-3 on HellaSwag. Fine-tuned FLAN and T0 baselines were preferred only about 27 to 30 percent of the time against SFT, while InstructGPT won about 73 percent, proving public sets did not match API use. Agreement among training labelers was about 72.6 percent and among held-out labelers about 77.3 percent, and reward models predicted held-out labeler choices at about 69.6 percent accuracy versus 72.4 percent in-group.

## Why this paper matters (what later work it unlocked — name the specific later papers among the 15 where relevant)
This paper matters because it proved that alignment can beat scale: a modest amount of human data plus compute, only about 60 petaflop-days for the 175-billion-parameter PPO model versus 3640 for GPT-3 pretraining, delivered more user value than making the model 100 times bigger. That finding justified the entire assistant era. Among the 15 papers, it builds directly on paper 03 on GPT-3 few-shot learning, which supplied the base models and the API prompt distribution. It directly unlocked paper 14 on DPO, which reframes the same human-preference data without an explicit reward model or PPO, paper 13 on LLaMA 2-Chat, which copies the SFT plus RLHF recipe with millions of preference pairs, paper 10 on Chinchilla in the sense that better data and alignment matter more than raw size, and paper 15 on DeepSeek-R1, which pushes the same reinforcement-learning-from-feedback idea further into reasoning with verifiable rewards and self-taught chains of thought.

## Honest limitations
InstructGPT still makes simple mistakes: it follows false premises instead of correcting them, hedges with long vague lists instead of giving a clear answer, and struggles when a prompt has many constraints like list ten 1930s movies set in France in a set number of sentences. It still invents facts, still produces toxic or biased or sexual content, and crucially it obeys harmful instructions because training prioritized helpfulness over harmlessness. The 40 labelers, mostly English-speaking contractors from the United States and Southeast Asia, cannot represent all users, and averaging their votes can silence minority concerns. Most comparisons were labeled only once for cost reasons, and public benchmarks do not capture real helpfulness, so safety requires much more than this first step.

## Fun trivia and history (from your web search)
InstructGPT was released quietly in January 2022 as a beta Playground option, and every time users switched to it they saw a notice that their prompts might be used for training. OpenAI insiders later said they did not want to oversell it as a big fundamental advance because it was just GPT-3.5 fine-tuned with human feedback plus a little dialogue data. Then ChatGPT launched on 30 November 2022 using almost the same RLHF recipe and exploded to over one million users in five days and a billion page views within months. Team members like John Schulman, Jan Leike, and Liam Fedus explained that benchmarks barely moved, but usability did, because the model finally inferred intent and held a conversation. That surprise hit turned the modest alignment report into the founding document of the chatbot age.

## Key terms explained (glossary of 5-8 terms)
Alignment means training a model to act in line with what users actually intend, summarized as helpful, honest, and harmless. RLHF means reinforcement learning from human feedback, where human judgments become a reward signal for tuning the model. SFT means supervised fine-tuning, simply imitating human-written ideal answers. Reward model means a separate neural network that reads a prompt plus answer and predicts a single quality score that mimics human preference. PPO means proximal policy optimization, a stable reinforcement-learning algorithm that improves the policy in small safe steps without jumping too far at once. KL penalty means a cost for drifting away from the original supervised model, keeping the tuned model grounded. Alignment tax means the drop in academic benchmark scores that sometimes happens after alignment tuning, which PPO-ptx largely fixed by mixing pretraining back in.

## Connections (which earlier paper it builds on, which later paper builds on it)
This paper builds on paper 03 on GPT-3, which provided the pretrained transformers, the few-shot prompting baseline, and the observation that scale alone does not guarantee obedience. It also builds on earlier summarization-from-feedback work that pioneered the three-stage SFT, reward-model, and PPO pipeline. Among later papers, it is the direct parent of paper 14 on DPO, which simplifies away the PPO step, paper 13 on LLaMA 2, which industrializes RLHF for open chat models, paper 09 on chain-of-thought, which was tested on InstructGPT variants like text-davinci-002 and benefits from instruction following, and paper 15 on DeepSeek-R1, which extends reinforcement learning from human preference to reasoning with rule-based rewards.
